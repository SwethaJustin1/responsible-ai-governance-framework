# 03 — Transparency Requirements

## Purpose

People affected by AI decisions have a right to understand what happened to them and why. Transparency is not a communications exercise — it is a governance obligation. This section defines what organisations must tell users, when, and in what form.

---

## When this section applies

All Level 2 and Level 3 systems. Any system where an individual receives a decision, score, recommendation, or outcome generated or influenced by AI.

---

## The three transparency obligations

### Obligation 1 — Existence disclosure
Users must know when AI is involved in a decision that affects them.

**What this means in practice:**
- Any customer-facing AI system must display a clear notice that AI is being used
- The notice must appear before the interaction, not buried in terms and conditions
- The notice must be in plain language — not "automated processing" but "this decision was made with the help of an AI system"
- The notice must be in the language the user is communicating in

**Minimum disclosure statement (adapt for context):**
"This [decision / recommendation / assessment] was generated with the assistance of an AI system. It was reviewed by [a human / our team] before being shared with you. If you have questions about how this decision was made, you can [contact us / request a review] at [channel]."

---

### Obligation 2 — Process disclosure
Users must be able to understand, in plain terms, what the AI system considered when making a decision about them.

**What this means in practice:**
- Prepare a plain-language explanation of what inputs the system uses
- Prepare a plain-language explanation of what the system does not consider — what context it cannot see
- This explanation does not need to reveal proprietary model details — it needs to tell users what information about them was used
- For Level 3 systems, this explanation must be available on request within 15 days

**Template explanation structure:**
"When assessing your [application / request / case], the system considered: [list of inputs in plain language]. The system did not have access to: [relevant context the user might expect was considered]. The decision was [made entirely by the system / recommended by the system and reviewed by a human]."

---

### Obligation 3 — Redress pathway
Every person affected by an AI decision must have a clear, accessible way to challenge it.

**What this means in practice:**
- A human review pathway must exist for all Level 2 and Level 3 decisions
- The pathway must be communicated at the time the decision is delivered — not only on request
- Response time commitment must be stated — recommended maximum 15 business days
- The human reviewer must have genuine authority to override the system — not merely to explain it
- The redress pathway must be accessible to users with limited digital access, language barriers, or disabilities

**What a redress pathway is not:**
- A link to a FAQ page
- An automated chatbot response
- A process that ends with "the system's decision is final"

---

## What to avoid

These phrases must not appear in any user-facing AI transparency communication:

| Do not say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "The algorithm decided" | "The AI system assessed" |
| "Automated decision — no appeal possible" | "You can request a human review" |
| "Our AI is unbiased" | "We test our AI for bias and publish the results" |
| "The system is 95% accurate" | "The system can make errors — here is how to flag one" |
| "We cannot explain how the AI works" | "Here is what information the system used about you" |

---

## Transparency checklist

- [ ] Existence disclosure in place and visible before interaction
- [ ] Plain-language process explanation prepared and available on request
- [ ] Redress pathway documented, communicated, and staffed
- [ ] Human reviewer has genuine override authority
- [ ] Response time commitment stated and tracked
- [ ] Disclosure reviewed by a plain-language editor before deployment
- [ ] Disclosure available in all languages used by the affected population
